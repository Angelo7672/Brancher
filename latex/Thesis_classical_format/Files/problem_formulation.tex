\chapter{Problem Formulation}
The goal of this thesis is to estimate the 3D motion of athletes from monocular drone footage.\\ 
Specifically, the task consists in predicting the 3D human pose of an athlete over time, represented as a sequence of 3D joint coordinates.
Given a video acquired from a UAV-mounted camera, the objective is to recover the spatio-temporal configuration of the human body despite challenging acquisition conditions such as motion blur, occlusions, and dynamic camera motion.


\section{Task Definition}
Let $x$ be a video sequence of an athlete captured by a drone-mounted monocular camera. 
The problem addressed in this thesis is to learn a function $f_\theta$, parameterized by $\theta$, that maps the input video to a sequence of 3D human poses:
\begin{equation}
    f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times C} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{T \times J \times (x,y,z)}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the temporal length of the video, $H$ and $W$ are the spatial dimensions of each frame, $C$ is the number of channels, $J$ denotes the number of body joints, and \((x,y,z)\) are the 3D coordinates of a joint.


\section{Input Representation}
The input to the model is a video sequence composed of $T$ consecutive RGB frames:
\begin{equation}
    x \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times C}
\end{equation}
The video is assumed to be captured by a moving drone, resulting in non-static backgrounds and varying viewpoints.


\section{Output Representation}
The output is a sequence of 3D human poses, one for each video frame. 
Each pose is represented as a set of $J$ 3D joint coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    y \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times J \times (x, y, z)}
\end{equation}
Each joint is represented by its $(x, y, z)$ coordinates in a root-relative reference frame, obtained by translating all joints such that the root joint is located at the origin.


\section{Learning Objective}
Given a training set of video sequences and corresponding ground-truth 3D poses, the learning objective is to minimize the discrepancy between predicted and ground-truth joint positions.
This is typically achieved by minimizing the MPJPE loss (see \ref{mpjpe}) over all joints and time steps:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MPJPE} = \frac{1}{T} \frac{1}{J} \sum_{t=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{J} \| \hat{\mathbf{y}_{t,j}} - \mathbf{y}_{t,j} \|_2
\end{equation}


\section{Challenges and Constraints}
Estimating 3D human pose from drone footage presents several challenges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Motion blur and low resolution}, caused by fast camera and subject motion;
    \item \textbf{Self-occlusions and partial visibility} of the human body;
    \item \textbf{Dynamic camera motion}, which prevents the use of static background assumptions;
    \item \textbf{Limited availability of labeled datasets}, as only a small number of public UAV-based 3D human pose datasets exist.
\end{itemize}
These factors make the problem significantly more challenging than standard 3D human pose estimation from fixed ground cameras.
